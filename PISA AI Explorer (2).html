What this data can tell you

Students were asked how often they use AI chatbots for four school tasks. For each answer group the file gives the share of students and their average PISA score. Comparing groups shows who uses AI and how their results differ. It does not show whether AI causes better or worse results.

Read every gap as an association, not an effect. Stronger students may simply use AI differently. The file has no standard errors, so gaps of a few points may not be meaningful.

How to use this site to draw your own lessons

  1. Start with the purpose, not the frequency. Compare “To help me learn” with “To draft texts for writing assignments” for the same country. The difference between purposes is the main story.
  2. Check both score types. Switch between Raw and ESCS adjusted. If a gap shrinks a lot, much of it reflects students’ socioeconomic background rather than AI use.
  3. Compare against both benchmarks. The EU and ECA averages often show opposite patterns. Check which one your country resembles.
  4. Look at the whole curve. Daily minus never hides the middle. Use the line charts to see if weekly users do best.
  5. Look at usage and scores together. A country can have high use and a small score gap, or low use and a large one. The Usage distribution tab and the Ranking table show both.

About the data
  • Source: uploaded file PISA AI Four Question Panel ECA EU.xlsx (sheet “Data”, 760 rows). Items ST438Q01 to ST438Q04. The file does not state the PISA cycle.
  • ECA average is the simple (unweighted) mean of the 17 systems in this file; checked against the file values. For math and reading it covers 16 systems, since Uzbekistan has no math or reading scores in the file.
  • QUA is shown as “Ukrainian regions”. This is an inference, not confirmed: OECD uses Q codes for sub national participants, and Ukraine was assessed in selected regions only (17 regions in PISA 2025, per Ukraine’s Ministry of Education). Please confirm with your data source.
  • The file has no standard errors, so differences shown here are not tested for statistical significance.
  • “ESCS adjusted” refers to scores adjusted for the PISA index of economic, social and cultural status, as labelled in the file.
